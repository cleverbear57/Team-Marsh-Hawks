FUTURE PRODUCT
Create California Rotation Lab for farmers and advisors. Its purpose is to explore plausible crop-rotation scenarios and learn soil/water/productivity tradeoffs. Support a California map pin, an editable local soil profile, and free choice from a supported annual-crop catalog. Show local suitability and data coverage; unsupported crops remain explicitly unmodeled. Orchards and vineyards belong to later crop-system modules, not annual replacement of established trees. Allow 5, 10, 15 or 20 years, planning a sequence before weather is revealed and revising future choices. Select a priority: soil protection, water conservation or crop output. Show all outcomes regardless of priority.

A repeatable judge demo runs three years with a fixed seed/weather sequence. Allow Finish Now after any completed year. Produce a priority-related outcome such as soil builder or water saver, a comparison report and a short learning reflection even on early finish; label early results preliminary. Never manufacture a positive outcome if productivity or evidence is inadequate. Use the same weather realization for all compared strategies.

DATA AND PROVENANCE
Discover NASA products through https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/ and follow official links to their data services. Download raw response bytes, catalog metadata separately, request parameters, coordinates, dates, version, units, spatial/temporal resolution, QA, missing values, access restrictions, citation and checksum. Distinguish satellite retrieval, satellite-plus-model product, reanalysis, ground observation, soil-map estimate, farmer input and game simulation. Raw samples at a few locations do not establish statewide field coverage. Preserve actual acquisition timestamps. Do not pool non-overlapping samples as though they were a matched validation dataset.

Use NASA POWER historical air temperature, daily extrema, humidity, wind, radiation and precipitation for climate context; its meteorology includes reanalysis and is not an on-farm weather station. Use SMAP for regional moisture context. Document whether a returned field is volumetric moisture or normalized wetness: never relabel wetness as m3/m3. Root-zone SMAP L4 is a modeled/assimilated estimate. Use GPM IMERG for rainfall; distinguish rates from accumulated rainfall and apply time-interval integration correctly. Use ECOSTRESS for LST and modeled ET, with cloud/quality flags and version-specific variables. Air temperature differs from land-surface temperature, and ET includes transpiration, evaporation and interception. If evaporation components are unavailable, report that gap. Use MODIS/HLS/Landsat vegetation history as a qualified vegetation-cover signal; low NDVI alone does not establish a bare field or its cause. GRACE/GRACE-FO measures broad terrestrial water storage; groundwater inference requires removing other storage components and uncertainty. NASADEM/SRTM supplies terrain, not parcel erosion loss.

Keep native resolution, pixel footprint, CRS and uncertainty. A common grid must not suggest that 9-km information became field precision. Aggregate with justified methods, keep categorical masks intact, distinguish interpolation from added evidence, and retain native timestamps before seasonal aggregation.

LOCAL SOIL, CROPS AND EXPERTISE
Use NRCS SSURGO/Soil Data Access or SoilWeb to retrieve actual local map-unit components and horizons, with texture, organic matter, pH, drainage, salinity, available-water capacity and depth. Retain component percentages, map identifiers and missing values. These are mapped estimates; farmer soil tests can override with documented date/depth. Do not invent a soil test or current rotation from a pin.

Use UC ANR, UCCE, UC IPM, local RCDs and official crop reports for evidence. Maintain crop profiles with region, season/planting/harvest windows, crop family, rooting, water requirements, nitrogen/residue behavior, heat-sensitive growth stages, drought/flood/frost susceptibility, pest/disease host relationships and locally supported rotation intervals. Every numeric threshold/coefficient needs a source, units, applicable conditions and confidence. Leave unsupported values null. Cover crops can consume spring water; species and termination timing matter. Legumes do not guarantee a fixed nitrogen credit. Different crop families can share a pathogen host. Expert publications are evidence; do not imply local advisors reviewed this tool unless they actually did.

MANDATORY STRATEGY COMPARISON
Compare at least THREE DISTINCT plans: A = the farmer's actual existing rotation and management; B = a locally justified cover-crop modification; C = a diversified cash/cover rotation. Preserve A even if it already uses good conservation practices. If A is unknown, ask for it or use an explicitly illustrative baseline. Do not assume monoculture, depletion or groundwater dependence. Keep farm area, starting soil, weather, irrigation constraints and economic basis consistent. User custom plans may add a fourth option. Offer sourced regional reference examples without declaring them universally suitable. Explain why each candidate suits the chosen priority, supporting evidence, benefits, risks, constraints and uncertainty; allow ties, tradeoffs and insufficient-evidence outcomes.

ANALYSIS REQUIREMENTS
1. Temperature/heat: air-temperature extrema and crop-stage exposure; use locally supported thresholds, not one universal crop injury threshold.
2. Rain/extremes: timing, dry spells, heavy rain and consecutive dry years. Historical resampling is a scenario, not a forecast. Preserve seasonality, correlated variables and multi-year drought persistence; document any explicit climate-change scenario separately.
3. Moisture/evaporation: track water inputs, irrigation, ET, runoff and drainage consistently; avoid double counting observed moisture and simulated storage. Record uncertainty and missing components.
4. Bare-field periods: use planting/harvest/residue/cover calendars and QA-screened vegetation evidence. Dead residue cover is not reliably represented by green NDVI. Report days/season windows and confidence.
5. Vulnerability: combine local crop suitability, phenology, weather exposure and water/soil constraints; explain drivers without diagnosing field disease from satellites.
6. Pests/disease: use specific host and rotation-break evidence. No generic diversity bonus presented as verified disease control.
7. Economics: include supplemental income, local markets/buyers, machinery/labor/establishment/termination costs and price/yield variability. County averages and sample budgets are not farm profit forecasts; report year, geography, units and assumptions, gross revenue versus net margin, and market-access gaps.
8. Mapping: California boundaries/counties, farm pin, soils and source pixel footprints/coverage. Verify location and agricultural suitability. State/county geometry is not a field boundary.
9. Interaction: future app allows crop, management, priority, horizon and scenario changes with comparable results. Implement these controls when this prompt is used for the implementation task.

MODELING AND RESULTS
Explicitly document the simulation/statistical approach: input classification, equations, assumptions, parameter sources, temporal step, state variables, uncertainty and validation. The checklist's term 'SI' is undefined; do not silently infer a special AI method. Use consistent units and describe the simulation/statistical method in plain language. No AI model is necessary to rank transparent tradeoffs.

A defensible first model can use a soil-water balance with separately reported terms, a crop-calendar cover indicator and a documented organic-matter proxy. No invented percentage improvement each year, automatic compounded soil-health gains, unsupported yield-loss curve or unexplained score. Unknown quantitative behavior should remain qualitative/unmodeled. Validate models with local observations/expert evidence before claiming predictive accuracy. Use sensitivity ranges/scenario ensembles rather than unjustified precision. All strategies get identical weather draws and initial conditions; random seeds must be reproducible.

Show observed weather/moisture context separately from modeled soil moisture, ground cover and organic-matter trends. Add crop-performance/water outcomes and side-by-side reference/current rotation comparisons. Explain what changed, why, costs and risks, uncertainty, information gaps, and what extra ground measurements would improve confidence. The ending asks the player to identify one tradeoff learned. Results support learning and discussion, not guaranteed recommendations.

DECISIONS FILLED IN FOR THE FIRST VERSION
These are product/engineering defaults, not observed agricultural facts. Use them without further design interviews unless a material conflict appears.

1. NAME, AUDIENCE AND PRESENTATION
Use “California Rotation Lab” as the product name and “Team Marsh Hawks” as the team credit. Existing repository UI may say “Rotation Compass”; unify visible naming when implementing. Serve farmers and advisors with a friendly, professional farm-planning game. Use a field illustration, seasonal timeline and clear charts. Avoid a complex 3D world. Use warm neutral backgrounds, readable dark text, green/blue/amber accents, text labels in addition to color, and reduced-motion support. Essential controls must work by keyboard and touch; crop drag-and-drop needs select/move-button equivalents. Put citations and technical details in expandable evidence panels, keeping sources available near results.

2. BUILD AND DELIVERY
The inspected repository uses index.html, style.css, data.js, engine.js and app.js. Start by checking their current state. Prefer its existing lightweight JavaScript approach; extract pure simulation/data functions into modules as necessary. Keep dependencies modest, pin any introduced versions and provide local run/build instructions. Serve files over HTTP rather than assuming fetch works under file://. No accounts, subscriptions, multiplayer, leaderboard, external messaging or automatic public deployment are required. A static bundle should support the cached demo. Store secrets only on a server or in an authorized local acquisition environment; never embed Earthdata credentials in browser JavaScript.

3. DATA PACKAGE INTEGRATION
Inspect california_rotation_data/README.md, REQUIREMENTS_COVERAGE.csv, FILE_INVENTORY.csv and all relevant source manifests. Preserve original raw data. Generate compact, versioned browser assets in a distinct derived/build-data folder, recording transformations and checksums. Do not ship the entire research ZIP or large national source archives to every browser.

The package contains POWER daily data for eight representative locations from 2001–2025, SMAP and GPM numerical service samples, SSURGO point/horizon data, California boundaries, DWR reservoir records, UC crop guidance, disease references and historical budget reports. Some NASA missions have metadata only. Verify file contents and coverage before use. Eight weather points and eight soil sample locations do not mean complete statewide coverage; sample sets have different coordinates and must be joined spatially and by source identifiers.

Use a data-status object with states such as available, partial, unavailable and failed. Distinguish cached observation from a game estimate. A failed API request must not silently produce synthetic observations or a green “live NASA” badge. For public APIs, use caching, bounded retries, visible timeouts and appropriate rate limits. Keep the demo available from cached real observations and explicitly labeled illustrative inputs. Maintain exact source date range and data version in the run record.

4. LOCATION AND SOIL SETUP
Offer map click, coordinate entry and a searchable list of California demonstration locations. Validate points against the California state polygon, not only its rectangular bounding box. Require a land point for the farm scenario. An in-state location is not automatically agricultural land or suitable for every crop.

Default demonstration point: Five Points, California, latitude 36.36, longitude -120.10. This is an illustrative sampling point, not an identified real farm or verified field boundary. Show county and data footprint. Use WGS84 longitude/latitude for interchange. Clearly label approximate pixel/soil-map resolution. Administrative boundaries and a location pin must not be displayed as a surveyed field polygon.

For an arbitrary point, fetch a matching soil map profile when access is available. Otherwise offer manual soil entry or an explicitly selected sample farm. Never silently reuse the nearest city's soil as the selected field's measured condition. Show component alternatives and percentages. Default to the dominant mapped component only when known and disclose that choice. Retain horizon depths and use depth-weighted calculations for a stated analysis depth; do not average all horizons equally. Only require values needed by the enabled model. Unknown inputs remain unknown. Farmer overrides record source, date and depth. Use per-hectare calculations internally with acre/hectare display choice; farm area is optional and required only for total money/water quantities.

5. BASELINE AND CROPS
Ask for the actual current crop SEQUENCE, not only the current crop name. Capture crop dates, cover/fallow periods, residue/tillage practices and irrigation constraints where known. If a user has no baseline, offer a clearly labeled example and preserve that designation in every comparison.

Initial crop-catalog candidates: processing tomato, wheat, dry bean, rice, lettuce and broccoli, plus locally evidenced cereal and legume cover options. Candidate membership does not imply that a complete validated model already exists. Each profile has a stable ID, family, supported regions, planting/harvest windows, duration, crop stages, water/heat sensitivity, rooting, cover/residue behavior, host relationships, economic evidence and per-field citations/confidence. Separate cash crops, cover crops and fallow/residue choices. Rice needs its own water-management treatment; exclude quantitative rice simulation until that treatment is supported. Established orchards/vineyards are future modules. Crop options with insufficient coefficients remain selectable for qualitative exploration with affected outputs explicitly unavailable.

Players freely assemble plans from supported annual-crop options. Highlight local fit without forcing a single rotation. Explain poor-fit selections. Reject impossible overlapping crop dates on the same single field; permit sequential seasons when a local calendar supports them. Version one models one representative field at a time. Do not assume one crop per calendar year: represent fall-planted crops and winter cover crops across year boundaries. Allow repeated sequences and copy/paste of planned years so a 20-year plan does not require 20 manual selections.

6. FLOW AND GAME STATE
Screens: Welcome → Farm setup → Rotation planner/comparison → Play years → Results/debrief. Provide Back navigation without discarding work. Regular horizon choices are 5, 10, 15 and 20 years, default 5. Require a priority selection; demo priority defaults to soil protection and is editable before play.

Represent a run with run ID, configuration version, location, soil snapshot, baseline, strategies, priority, planned horizon, completed years, seed, weather-source identifiers, decisions and output availability flags. Use distinct states for setup, ready, running-year, between-years and finished. Snapshot all inputs at launch. Before launch, edits recalculate all candidates. During play, only unplayed years may change. Past years are immutable unless the user explicitly restarts or creates a new run. Record revisions rather than silently changing history.

Each turn: review upcoming crop/management → confirm → reveal seasonal weather → show crop and soil consequences with explanation → choose Continue, edit future plan, or Finish Now. No timer pressure. Prevent duplicate advance clicks. Finish Now becomes available after one completed year. At zero completed years show “no simulated outcome yet” rather than award a badge. A finish reports actual completed years, planned horizon and early/full completion state.

7. COMPARISONS AND PRIORITIES
Always display at least three distinct plans. The actual farmer baseline is A. B changes cover management where feasible; C changes the cash/cover sequence. A user-custom strategy can be D. Show exactly what differs in crop timing and management. If B or C duplicates A, derive another locally supported alternative or state that a valid third plan needs more input; never count duplicate plans as three distinct strategies.

Show paired differences from A for available metrics. Keep soil, water, productivity and economic results visible. Priority affects explanation order and preference among supported tradeoffs, not physical model outputs. Prefer a transparent multi-criteria/Pareto comparison: say when one plan improves a goal but worsens another. Avoid unexplained weighted overall scores and universal “best” claims. Missing output is never treated as zero, success or failure.

8. WEATHER AND TIME
Use daily observed/reanalysis input sequences where available, with seasonal/annual display summaries. Index each location's quality-checked historical record; do not generate uncorrelated daily temperature/rain values. Use seeded contiguous year blocks or another documented block-resampling method so seasons and multi-year dry periods remain coherent. Persist original source-year mapping. Exclude incomplete periods or label them and state the handling method. Sample sequences describe historical variability, not future climate probabilities.

Default normal mode samples a new seed each new run; Replay Same Weather reuses a saved seed. Use identical weather and assumptions across all comparison plans. Explicit hotter/drier stress scenarios may perturb inputs using a documented user-chosen amount, but label these constructed scenarios; do not claim a climate projection or probability. Changes trigger a fresh comparison/run version. No arbitrary crop-damage penalty from a random event card: event descriptions summarize the actual weather inputs.

9. SCIENTIFIC MODEL CONTRACT
Separate climate/observations, crop calendars, soil-water estimates, organic-matter estimates, economics and outcome logic. A candidate daily storage balance is S_next = S + precipitation + effective irrigation - ET - runoff - drainage. Every term must have consistent depth units and documented estimation. Enforce conservation with bounded storage and explicitly accounted excess/deficit; clipping alone must not hide water. Do not assimilate observed moisture and then count the same precipitation again without a documented assimilation scheme.

Derive water capacity from supported soil horizons and rooted depth, preserving uncertainty. Crop ET needs an evidenced method and crop-stage inputs. If coefficients or evaporation components are missing, show qualitative water demand and unavailable quantitative totals. Report moisture storage, normalized wetness and volumetric water content as different quantities.

For organic matter, use a documented calibrated response model only where parameter evidence supports it. Otherwise display a clearly labeled qualitative/proxy trajectory with assumptions and sensitivity; never render that proxy as measured percent organic matter or convert it to tonnes of carbon without bulk density, depth and a supported conversion. Soil health cannot be inferred from soil moisture alone. Show observed, mapped, farmer-entered and simulated values distinctly.

Add locally supported heat-stage exposure, water-stress and pest host/rotation constraints without inventing universal damage percentages. Do not promise nitrate reductions from SMAP/ECOSTRESS. A crop can reduce one risk and increase another. Any ensemble/sensitivity range must disclose what varies and must not be called a confidence interval without statistical justification. If a model lacks calibration, describe it as an educational model and document limitations.

10. OUTCOMES AND LEARNING
Show soil moisture context, ground-cover/bare-window estimates, organic-matter trajectory status, water demand/use, crop performance availability and economic tradeoffs. Distinguish percentage-point changes from percent changes and per-area from farm totals. Charts share units and time axes. Show missing intervals as gaps.

Outcome categories include Soil Builder, Water Saver, Harvest Resilience, Mixed Tradeoffs and Insufficient Evidence. Evaluate evidence-supported improvement against the baseline on the chosen priority and show productivity implications. Use only documented criteria; report ties. Do not award Water Saver because a failed crop consumed less water without explaining the production loss. Do not claim Harvest Resilience from one weather realization alone. Early runs use “early indicator” wording; missing productivity/model evidence prevents a strong success claim. Explain the rule and the actual observed/model outputs behind each outcome.

End with: what changed; what choices/weather drove it; comparison with existing rotation; benefits/costs/risks; uncertain or missing information; a locally sourced reference; and one short reflection question about a tradeoff. Feedback should explain why an answer fits the scenario, not mark all answers correct. Export a concise results summary plus CSV/JSON including configuration, seed, provenance and caveats. Keep results available on screen without requiring export.

11. DEMO AND RESILIENCE
Provide a one-click “Judge Demo — 3 years” using cached real data and labeled illustrative management inputs. Default to the Five Points sample and a sourced regional annual-rotation example, without claiming the sample represents the landowner's actual history. Fix seed 57 and store the chosen source-year IDs so later data changes do not change the demonstration. Select the actual three source years after inspecting available records; do not invent weather observations. Show source-year labels in the evidence drawer. Keep loading time low, permit future-plan changes and Finish Now, and generate the same report as regular mode with preliminary labels. Target a roughly two-minute guided demonstration through one crop change, one weather reveal and the outcome report.

Allow save/resume locally with a versioned save format, and export/import a run. If local storage is unavailable, keep the current session working and explain the limitation. Ask before clearing a saved run. Do not upload precise farm locations or soil tests automatically. Provide visible loading, empty, failed-source and unsupported-model states. Missing map tiles should fall back to coordinates and local boundaries; missing remote data should leave the cached demo usable.

12. VALIDATION AND ACCEPTANCE
Check the actual code rather than assuming an existing UI label means a feature works. Use meaningful tests for deterministic seeds, paired weather equality, baseline preservation, plan edits affecting future years only, horizon choices, early finish, reset/replay, missing-data propagation, map-boundary rejection, soil units/depth handling and water conservation where modeled. Verify crop overlap rules and that unsupported coefficients yield unavailable outputs. Test exports and persistence with matching run IDs/provenance. Exercise the three-year cached demo with network disabled, keyboard-only operation, and a small viewport. Record outcomes honestly; functional tests do not validate agronomic accuracy.

A requirement is complete only when its data inputs, behavior, explanation and validation evidence are present. Maintain a 21-row acceptance matrix using the supplied checklist. If numeric ET, crop response, vegetation data or model calibration remains unavailable, document the limitation and implement an honest unavailable/qualitative state; do not manufacture data to mark a requirement complete. The farmer-priority selector and model claims must agree with actual behavior.
